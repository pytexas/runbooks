    Venue Committee Runbook¶

    Responsibilities: venue relationship and contract logistics, A/V vendor, catering and food service, evening events, alcohol compliance (TABC permit and peace officers), swag ordering, badges, linens, signage, registration desk day-of, volunteers.

    See venue.md, av.md, catering.md, network-event.md, volunteers.md, day-of-operations-guide.md, day-of-supplies.md.

    Month -13 to -12: Venue Contract¶

    Next year's venue contract is signed immediately after the current conference; the Conference Chair leads this with Venue Committee support. See venue.md for contract mechanics and next-year-planning.md for the workstream.

    • Review this year's venue experience (what worked, capacity, pricing, acoustics)
    • Conflict-check candidate dates against the Austin events spreadsheet (Easter, Passover, Texas Relays, Cap10k, SXSW proximity, graduations)
    • Sign the facility use agreement and pay the 50% deposit at signing
    • Document the rationale for next year

    Month -7 to -3: A/V Vendor¶

    See av.md for the deep dive.

    • Month -7: start vendor outreach (current vendor: Garner Media)
    • Month -6 to -5.5: request the quote; confirm scope covers recording, livestream, and captioning
    • Review the contract and sign with Conference Chair signoff
    • Pay the deposit; coordinate the schedule with Finance (deposit at booking, installment near the event, final after)

    Month -5.5: Ticket Timeline and Registration Setup¶

    • Set the ticket timeline with Finance: early bird opens Dec 1, speaker list ~week of Jan 7, early bird closes mid-January
    • Start Pretix setup, including demographic questions
    • Configure shirts as a pre-order add-on alongside tickets

    Month -4 to -3: Alcohol Compliance Starts¶

    Any alcohol service needs a TABC permit and two hired licensed peace officers (a city requirement; two are budgeted). Both are long-lead and fragile. Peace officer hiring can take a month of bounced emails and often closes through a personal contact. TABC approval is slow; a permit submitted a few weeks out can still be in review the day before the conference.

    • Start peace officer outreach now; expect bounced contacts and dead ends
    • Identify the TABC permit requirements and application lead time
    • Agree on the worst case with the Chair in advance ("worst case no alcohol")

    Week -11 to -8: Catering and Swag Ideation¶

    See catering.md.

    • Build the catering ideas doc with headcount assumptions (~50-75 Friday dinner, ~150 Saturday network event)
    • Build swag ideation docs (shirt ideas, other swag, speaker gifts)
    • Gather catering, bartending, and swag quotes (~8-10 weeks out); remind vendors the 501(c)(3) status removes sales tax
    • Week -8.5: confirm catering deposits and headcounts (deposits roughly half of totals)
    • Book the network event venue (see network-event.md)

    Week -6.5 to -4.5: Orders and Plans¶

    • Build the catering plan sheet (~6 weeks out): per-day blocks (breakfast, coffee, snacks, all-day beverages) plus a "who's getting what" shopping tab with an owner per item
    • Pay the first swag order (patches, stickers, pins) ~6.5 weeks out
    • Pay both dinner-venue deposits
    • Close shirt pre-orders ~5 weeks out (Marketing runs the last-chance campaign); place the shirt order immediately after
    • Final catering orders due ~4.5 weeks out
    • Order linens from American Party Rental (the library provides none; reuse last year's invoice)
    • Confirm the peace officers are secured
    • Start collecting dietary restrictions from registration; collecting them in the final week is too late
    • Order badges from ConferenceBadges.com (template, print ahead plus day-of printing). Badge orders tend to slip to the final two weeks; order them here and beat the pattern

    Week -3.5 to -2.5: Compliance and Final Swag¶

    • Submit the TABC permit if not already in (see the warning above; this is the last responsible moment, not the target)
    • All swag ordered and approved
    • Finalize both dinner plans
    • Raise the hotel room block question with the Chair and get a decision; this question tends to drift unresolved

    Week -2 to -1: Signage and Final Prep¶

    • Plan signage on a Miro board: ~6 standing signs, 5-10 single-page; check whether the library owns sign holders
    • Print signage
    • Confirm venue access times (Friday-Sunday, 6:00 AM to 7:00 PM; there is no Thursday access)
    • Ask the library for reserved organizer parking (the parking attendant said reserving is routine)
    • Pre-conference walkthrough with the venue contact
    • Final head counts to caterers; write down each year's delivery windows (breakfast before doors, afternoon during the mid-afternoon break)
    • Decide the leftover-food policy in advance (standing policy: give to library staff)
    • Confirm A/V on-site hours all 3 days
    • Gather supplies and emergency kit (see day-of-supplies.md)
    • Confirm dietary restrictions are collected for the Friday dinner and checked for Saturday

    Registration Desk and Volunteers¶

    See registration.md and volunteers.md.

    • Plan the three-station flow: check-in, badge pickup, shirt pickup by poker chip
    • 2 on check-in laptops, 1-2 on badges; lanyards on a stand separate from badges; 3-table bubble layout leaves room for backpacks
    • Test check-in end-to-end
    • Plan edge cases: name not in system, late add, sponsor/comp tickets, day-2 badge loss (stock blank badges plus a label printer and loaner lanyards)
    • Recruit volunteers via email, social, prior years, student orgs; 2-3 hour shifts with coverage plus backup
    • Brief volunteers before doors; give them an identifier (shirt or badge)

    The registration bot fails on corporate bulk purchases (registering email vs attendee email); have a manual override ready.

    Conference Days¶

    See day-of-operations-guide.md for the full run of day. Setup happens Friday morning in parallel with tutorials; there is no Thursday access.

    • Photograph venue property at load-in (and again at load-out) to avoid damage disputes
    • Friday: 6:00 AM venue access, then coffee pickup and registration, food, and linens setup before doors; Costco + HEB supply run during the morning tutorial
    • Reg desk set before doors on Saturday and Sunday
    • Catering deliveries before doors; tear down food after each window (food out for an hour is food gone)
    • Two food lines, clearly marked; people miss the second one, so sign it or post a volunteer
    • Lunch: post the restaurant map on a slide before dismissal; captains with flags lead groups
    • Each evening: pack leftovers (to library staff), secure equipment, brief the next day

    Post-Conference¶

    Week 1¶

    • Return equipment (photograph condition)
    • Process final vendor invoices with Finance
    • Thank volunteers, venue staff, A/V, caterers

    Vendor Evaluation¶

    • A/V: equipment, support, responsiveness, value, use again?
    • Catering: food quality, dietary handling, timing, value, use again?
    • Venue: space, staff, value, issues, use again?
    • Document for next year

    Retrospective¶

    • Committee retro: what worked, what didn't, action items
    • Update this runbook
    • Update the vendor contact list with notes
    • Update catering quantities from counted leftovers
    • Archive setup photos

    Food Quantity Calibration¶

    Count leftovers every year and adjust.

    • Bananas: budget about one per two to three attendees per day (about 40-50 lb at full capacity)
    • Diet and zero sodas, sparkling water, and fruit are the hits
    • Muffins and kolaches tend to be over-ordered; order fewer
    • Buy light on Friday to gauge preferences, then bulk for the weekend
    • Order ice online; store runs are the fallback restock and eat organizer time
    • Coffee is the largest catering friction and budget overrun

    See catering.md for the full plan-sheet structure.

    Cross-Committee¶

    • Conference: operations updates, escalations, major decisions
    • Marketing: signage, shirt last-chance campaign, swag distribution
    • Speaker: A/V needs, tech checks, timing
    • Finance: vendor payments, headcounts, budget tracking, insurance deadline (Week -4.5)
    • Community: registration data, attendee comms, volunteer recruitment

    Emergency Procedures¶

    These are untested defaults, not procedures proven at a past conference. The venue's own emergency procedures come first; escalate to the Conference Chair.

    Medical:

    • Call 911, notify the Conference Chair
    • Provide assistance if trained
    • Have someone meet EMTs at the entrance
    • Document

    Fire or evacuation:

    • Follow venue procedures
    • Announce calmly, direct to exits
    • Account for organizers
    • Don't re-enter until cleared

    Catering no-show:

    • Notify the Conference Chair
    • Emergency Costco or grocery run
    • Communicate the delay

    Pitfalls¶

    • Poker chips for shirt distribution work but need pre-sorting or chips placed inside badges; keep the shirt table staffed; say loudly whether a shirt comes with the ticket.
    • Do not let the print vendor alter the logo.
    • Coffee runs over budget every year; it is the largest catering friction.
    • Food out for an hour is food gone, except the forbidden item of the year.
    • Hallway echo is loud enough to ring heads; sound deadening is an open problem.
    • Power strips at every tutorial table; missing power is a recurring complaint.
    • Tech checks are Friday (before 9:00 or at lunch), not a separate setup day.
    • The phone-numbers roster in the Org Info Sheet beats chat in emergencies.
