# Speaker Committee Runbook

The Speaker Committee owns the CFP, talk and keynote selection, tutorials, the schedule, speaker communications, and day-of speaker coordination.

See `cfp.md`, `keynote.md`, `run-of-show.md`, and `conference-master-timeline.md`.

## Month -12 to -8: Before Kickoff

- [ ] Recruit tutorial presenters by direct invitation, starting with hallway conversations at PyCon US (~11 months out)
- [ ] Review last year's CFP outcomes and topic gaps
- [ ] Read the last two retros for speaker-related action items

Tutorials are recruited by direct invitation, not through the open CFP.
Plan on two community presenters plus a possible sponsored workshop.
Aim to confirm the first tutorial presenter in person at PyCon, eleven months out.

## Month -8 (Mid-August): Kickoff

- [ ] Open the keynote nomination form (Google Form) at the kickoff meeting
- [ ] Set the CFP open date (October 1) as the cycle's first hard deadline
- [ ] Set the rest of the CFP timeline: close early December, review at least 2 full weeks over the holidays, notify around the new year

## Month -7 (September): CFP Prep

- [ ] Update CFP questions in Pretalx: title, abstract, description, level, length, bio, photo, requirements, recording consent, CoC agreement
- [ ] Add a travel-assistance question (feeds opportunity grant planning; see `grants.md`)
- [ ] Test submission end-to-end
- [ ] Line up reviewers and brief them on the rubric and deadlines
- [ ] Prepare the past-speaker CFP invitation (a per-year Google Group of past speakers exists for this)
- [ ] Coordinate announcement graphics and copy with Marketing
- [ ] Close keynote nominations end of September

## Month -6.5 (October 1): CFP Opens

- [ ] Open the CFP in Pretalx
- [ ] Send direct invitations to the past-speaker Google Group
- [ ] Announce with Marketing: newsletter, social, Discord, python.org events, pythondeadlin.es
- [ ] Run the keynote organizer election (ranked ballot, each candidate scored +2 to -2)

The CFP runs October 1 to early December, a 9-10 week window.
A short extension is an option if volume is low near the deadline.
Expect a few dozen submissions.

### Keynote Invite Order

- [ ] Compile election results into a rankings doc and decide the ordered invite list (late October)
- [ ] Work down the list in order; be ready to go deep

Invitations often go several names deep before someone accepts.
The ranked backup list is the plan, not a contingency.
See `keynote.md` for outreach mechanics.

## Month -5 to -4: CFP Close and Review

- [ ] Mid-window reminder push with Marketing; final-week countdown before close
- [ ] Close the CFP early December
- [ ] Reviewers vote in Pretalx over at least 2 full weeks
- [ ] Hold an in-person decision meeting to pick the lineup and build an ordered waitlist
- [ ] Send the speaker information form to accepted speakers at acceptance time (mid-December): name, socials, photo, logistics

Target roughly 14 talks for the single track and keep an ordered waitlist of about 10 with the top few flagged.

Collect speaker name, socials, and photo at acceptance, not later.
Response rates crater once the acceptance moment passes, and Marketing will need the data before speakers get around to it.

## Month -3.5 (Around January 1): Notifications

- [ ] Send acceptances with a hard confirmation deadline (~2 weeks)
- [ ] Keep the waitlist ordered with the top candidates flagged
- [ ] Reject everything below the waitlist promptly
- [ ] Offer standby speakers free tickets
- [ ] Hand speaker data to Marketing for the reveal campaign (~January 15)

Give Marketing the actual headshot and schedule files, never links into Pretalx; those links break later and take the site down with them.

Late dropouts happen even after the schedule is final, sometimes just weeks before the conference.
Hold ordered alternates and keep slack in the schedule.

## Week -11 to -7: Schedule

- [ ] Confirm all tutorials
- [ ] Build the draft schedule: 30-minute blocks (25-minute talk plus 5 minutes of combined Q&A and next-speaker setup), keynote (~45 minutes) after opening remarks, a 20-minute sponsored talk right before lunch
- [ ] Slot sponsor pitches with Finance (by tier, spaced across the days)
- [ ] Present the proposed schedule to the organizers (~Week -8)
- [ ] Publish the schedule (the PSF grant application requires a published schedule; see `finance-committee-runbook.md`)
- [ ] Get Slido and StageTimer single-event quotes (~Week -7.5)

Remote talks are high-risk; an audio failure on one draws more criticism than anything else in the program.
If you accept one, rehearse it end-to-end with the A/V vendor and hold a standby talk.

## Week -2 to -1: Final Prep

- [ ] Send the speaker KBYG (~2 weeks out): slot, Discord speaker role, slide upload, tech-check windows, green room, Friday event details
- [ ] Open the slide-upload Drive folder; ask each speaker for a text file of technical terms to feed caption prep
- [ ] Stage backup dongles (Dell, Apple, HP); USB-C is the preferred connection
- [ ] Build per-day lightning talk signup forms (8-9 slots per day)
- [ ] Confirm the speaker RSVP count for the Friday appreciation dinner with Venue

Lightning talk submissions exceed slots every year; organizers curate an ordered subset from each day's form.

## Conference Days

### Friday

- [ ] Run speaker tech checks before 9:00 or at lunch (catch-as-catch-can between stage events on the weekend)
- [ ] Greet tutorial instructors and verify rooms: power strips at every table, projection, mic
- [ ] Check in on tutorials during breaks; thank instructors at end of day
- [ ] Evening: Friday appreciation dinner (speakers, sponsors, organizers, Friends of PyTexas); brief speakers in person here

Speakers don't read emails.
The KBYG is a reference; the in-person Friday briefing is the real briefing.

Tutorial rooms need power strips at every table; presenters notice when they're missing.

### Saturday and Sunday

Morning:

- [ ] Set up the green room; check it before each talk block
- [ ] Set up the on-deck table with signage near the stage
- [ ] Test A/V, StageTimer, and the livestream with the A/V and Community teams
- [ ] Backup dongles ready

Throughout:

- [ ] Speaker check-in on arrival with a walkthrough (slot, on-deck rule, mic, timer)
- [ ] On-deck rule: the next speaker sits at the table when the prior talk begins; tech check during the 5-minute changeover
- [ ] Time warnings via StageTimer; Q&A via Slido
- [ ] Never run early; keep Python trivia ready as filler
- [ ] Run lightning talks from the day's form (Saturday before close, Sunday ~4:45)

## Post-Conference

### Week +1 to +2

- [ ] Thank speakers (email, and social with Marketing)
- [ ] Distribute per-speaker feedback: compile Slido and feedback-form data into a per-speaker links doc
- [ ] Collect remaining slides (respect opt-outs)
- [ ] Check recording quality with the A/V vendor

### Later

- [ ] Recordings: the vendor delivers unlisted YouTube videos; each video and the playlist must be flipped public (see `av.md`)
- [ ] Add this year's speakers to the past-speaker Google Group for next year's CFP invites
- [ ] Update this runbook, the CFP questions, and the KBYG
- [ ] Archive proposals and reviews

```
We'd love to see you at PyTexas [DATES], and we hope you'll submit again next year.

Thanks again,
The PyTexas [YEAR] Speaker Committee
```

## Speaker KBYG Outline

- Conference details, dates, venue, your slot
- Discord invite and speaker role
- Slide upload folder and the technical-terms text file for caption prep
- Tech: USB-C preferred, your laptop, dongles on hand, demo needs
- Tech-check windows: Friday before 9:00 or at lunch
- The on-deck table rule
- Day-of: green room, timing (StageTimer), mic, Q&A via Slido
- Friday appreciation dinner (with RSVP count)
- Recording policy
- After your talk: slides, feedback, recording
- Code of conduct
- Travel and the opportunity grant program

## Cross-Committee

- Marketing: CFP campaign timing, speaker reveal and teasers, schedule on the site, post-event thank-yous
- Finance: opportunity grants (speakers get priority), sponsor pitch slots in the schedule
- Venue: A/V requirements, green room, Friday dinner headcount
- Community: Discord speaker roles, streaming, recording publishing

## Pitfalls

- Keep headshots and schedule assets out of Pretalx; the site must host its own copies.
- Speakers don't read emails; brief them in person at the Friday dinner.
- Never let the schedule run early; keep trivia filler ready.
- Enforce the on-deck table when the prior talk starts; tech check during the changeover.
