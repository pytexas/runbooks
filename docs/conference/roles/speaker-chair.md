# Speaker Chair

The Speaker Chair leads the Speaker Committee: the CFP, talk selection, keynotes, tutorials, the schedule, and all speaker communications.
This role absorbed the retired Program Chair.

## Responsibilities

### Pre-Event

* Open the keynote nomination form (Google Form) at kickoff and close it end of September.
* Run the keynote organizer election in early October: ranked ballot, +2 to -2 scoring, then a decided invite order. Work down the list and keep the backup order intact; the 2026 keynoters were #2 and #5 on the list.
* Open the CFP on Pretalx October 1, the cycle's first hard deadline, and close it early December.
* Ask in the CFP whether selected speakers will need travel assistance; the answers feed opportunity grant planning.
* Recruit tutorial presenters by direct invitation, starting with PyCon hallway conversations about 11 months out. Tutorials are not part of the open CFP.
* Run talk review over the holidays, at least 2 full weeks: voting in Pretalx, then an in-person decision meeting.
* Notify speakers around the new year with a hard confirmation deadline of about 2 weeks. Hold an ordered waitlist with the top candidates flagged, reject everything below the waitlist promptly, and offer standby speakers free tickets.
* Send the speaker information form at acceptance and collect name, socials, and photo then; chasing later is painful (2026: only 6 of about 15 had responded when marketing first checked).
* Publish the schedule with slack for late dropouts (2025: one about 2.5 weeks out) and present it to the planning meeting about 7.5 weeks out.
* Get Slido and StageTimer single-event quotes about 7.5 weeks out.
* Treat remote talks as high-risk and rehearse the audio path; the 2026 remote talk's audio failure was the single most-criticized program item.
* Send the speaker KBYG about 2 weeks out, open the slide-upload folder, and collect technical-term lists for caption prep.
* Build per-day lightning talk signup forms in the final week.

### Day of Event

* Brief speakers in person at the Friday appreciation dinner; speakers do not read email.
* Run speaker tech checks Friday before 9:00 or at lunch; catch-as-catch-can between stage events on the weekend.
* Keep the program on time with StageTimer and run Q&A through Slido. Never let the schedule run early; have filler trivia ready.
* Seat the on-deck speaker when the prior talk begins; each 30-minute block is a 25-minute talk plus 5 minutes of Q&A and changeover.
* Curate lightning talks: 8-9 slots per day, and submissions exceed slots.

### Post Event

* Distribute per-speaker feedback compiled from Slido and the feedback form.
* Feed speaker-experience lessons into the retro.
